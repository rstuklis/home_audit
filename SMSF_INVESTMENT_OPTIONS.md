# ASX-listed investment options for a 60-year-old SMSF in retirement phase (AUD 10-25k parcels)

Report date: 2026-09-06 (Australian financial year 2026-27). Profile: a hypothetical Australian resident aged 60, trustee and member of a Self-Managed Super Fund (SMSF) whose account is in retirement phase (an account-based pension), asking which Australian Securities Exchange (ASX)-listed investments can be bought through an ordinary online broker in parcels of roughly AUD 10,000 to 25,000.

**This document is general information only. It has been prepared without regard to any person's objectives, financial situation or needs and is not personal financial advice. Anyone considering a financial product should read the Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) and Target Market Determination (TMD) and consider obtaining advice from an Australian Financial Services (AFS) licensee or an SMSF specialist.**

## How to read this report

- This report was produced on 2026-09-06 by a multi-agent research pass: eight researchers (one per dimension), an adversarial refutation of every claim each produced, a fact-check plus hostile peer review of thirty candidate tickers, a completeness critic, two gap-fill passes and a synthesis, all run from a cloud container whose network policy allowed web search but blocked direct access to the ATO, ASX and issuer sites. That is why some items are marked "knowledge, not live-verified".

- Every statement was drawn from a research digest in which each claim was independently verified, corrected or refuted against sources as at the date shown. Claims marked **refuted** are not repeated; where a corrected version matters it is used. Claims marked **unverified** are flagged as uncertain in the text.
- Two dimensions of the underlying research (the regulatory-boundary dimension and the two "gap-fill" dimensions on foreign withholding tax and the regulatory/macro backbone) ran with no live web access and rest on model knowledge current to roughly June 2026. Their content is labelled "knowledge, not live-verified" wherever it is used.
- Product candidates carry a **skeptic score** (0-10) from a hostile review that attacked each product on redundancy, cost, concentration, sequence-of-returns risk, liquidity and pension-phase tax logic. A score is a statement of opinion about fit for this hypothetical profile, not a recommendation.
- All currency is AUD; percentages are per annum unless stated. "Units a AUD 10k / 25k parcel buys" is floor(parcel / approximate unit price) and is illustrative only, because unit prices move daily and several prices below are estimates.

## 1. The retirement-phase rules that matter (as at 2026-09-06)

All items in this section were verified against Australian Taxation Office (ATO) or legislative sources unless labelled otherwise.

**Tax on earnings.** Income and net capital gains on assets supporting a retirement-phase account-based pension are exempt current pension income (ECPI), taxed at 0% in the fund, versus 15% on income and an effective 10% on discounted capital gains (one-third Capital Gains Tax (CGT) discount after 12 months) in accumulation phase. ECPI is claimed under the segregated method (no actuarial certificate) or the proportionate method (actuary certifies the exempt percentage). Since 2021-22 a fund wholly in retirement phase for the whole year needs no actuarial certificate under either method. A fund with "disregarded small fund assets" (a member with total super balance (TSB) above AUD 1.6 million at the prior 30 June who is receiving a retirement-phase pension) cannot use the segregated method; the AUD 1.6 million is fixed in the legislation and is not indexed (knowledge, not live-verified).

**Franking credits.** Because the fund's tax rate on pension assets is 0%, franking credits attached to dividends and franked ETF or LIC distributions are refunded in cash by the ATO after the annual return is lodged. AUD 10,000 of dividends fully franked at the 30% company rate carries AUD 4,285.71 of credits (cash x 30/70); a 4.0% fully franked cash yield is 5.71% grossed-up; a 4.5% cash yield at 80% franked is 6.04% grossed-up. The fund must hold each parcel "at risk" for at least 45 days (90 for preference shares), excluding acquisition and disposal days; the AUD 5,000 small-shareholder exemption is available only to individuals. Refundability of excess franking credits to complying super funds is unchanged as at 2026-09-06.

**Foreign withholding tax (the mirror image; knowledge, not live-verified).** The foreign income tax offset (FITO) under Division 770 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 is non-refundable and cannot be carried forward, and the ATO's SMSF annual return instructions say a fund cannot claim a FITO on foreign tax paid on ECPI. A fund 100% in retirement phase therefore permanently loses withholding tax deducted inside international ETFs: about 15% on United States dividends under the treaty (roughly 0.18-0.20% of net asset value a year for an S&P 500 fund yielding 1.2-1.3%), roughly 0.22-0.28% a year for a developed-markets ex-Australia fund (VGS, BGBL, HGBL, VGAD) and about 0.20-0.25% for DHHF, which holds US-domiciled ETFs and so suffers a second, unreported layer on its non-US sleeves. VDHG holds Australian-domiciled Vanguard wholesale funds and carries a single layer. This drag is often larger than the ETF's management fee.

**Transfer balance cap (TBC).** The general TBC indexed from AUD 2.0 million to AUD 2.1 million on 1 July 2026 (ATO SMSF newsroom). A person starting a first retirement-phase pension on or after that date has a personal cap of AUD 2.1 million; existing pensioners receive only a proportional share of the AUD 100,000 increase based on unused cap space. Amounts above the cap stay in accumulation (15% tax) or are withdrawn. An excess accrues notional earnings at the general interest charge (GIC) rate (90-day bank bill yield plus 7 percentage points, compounding daily) and excess transfer balance tax is 15% on a first breach, 30% on subsequent breaches. The GIC rate for July-September 2026 was not verified; the 2025-26 first-quarter rate was 10.78% and the often-quoted 7.10% is a 2020 figure. GIC has been non-deductible since 1 July 2025.

**Minimum drawdown.** 4% of the 1 July balance for a member under 65 (5% at 65-74, 6% at 75-79, 7% at 80-84, 9% at 85-89, 11% at 90-94, 14% at 95+), rounded to the nearest AUD 10, pro-rated in the commencement year and nil if commenced 1-30 June. The temporary 50% reduction ended 30 June 2023. Pension payments to a member aged 60 or over from a taxed fund are tax-free. The payment must actually be made in cash from the fund by 30 June 2027; a journal entry does not count, and an in-specie transfer can only be a lump sum (for example a partial commutation), which since 1 July 2017 never counts towards the minimum. If the minimum is not paid, the pension is taken to have ceased from the start of the year and ECPI is lost, subject to a once-only self-assessed concession for shortfalls of no more than one-twelfth of the minimum caught up within 28 days of discovery.

**Condition of release at 60 (knowledge, not live-verified).** Under Superannuation Industry (Supervision) (SIS) Regulation 6.01(7), a member aged 60 or over is retired when an arrangement under which they were gainfully employed ends on or after age 60, with no requirement to intend never to work again; preservation age is 60 for everyone born on or after 1 July 1964. A transition to retirement income stream (TRIS) that has not converted to retirement phase is taxed at 15% inside the fund, is capped at a 10% drawdown and does not count against the TBC; the 0%-tax premise of this report assumes a retirement-phase account-based pension.

**Division 296.** The Treasury Laws Amendment (Building a Stronger and Fairer Super System) Act 2026 passed Parliament on 10 March 2026 and received royal assent on 13 March 2026. From 2026-27 an additional 15% applies to the share of realised earnings attributable to TSB between AUD 3 million and AUD 10 million, and 25% additional (40% headline) above AUD 10 million; thresholds are indexed in AUD 150,000 and AUD 500,000 steps. Earnings are realised (taxable-income based, unrealised gains excluded) but ECPI is added back, so pension-phase earnings count. SMSFs may make an irrevocable all-or-nothing election to reset CGT cost bases to market value at 30 June 2026 by the due date of the 2026-27 annual return; first assessments issue in 2027-28. The tax is levied on the individual, not the fund, and franking credits do not reduce it. Because a retirement-phase account is bounded by the TBC (about AUD 2.1 million), Division 296 matters only where total super balance across pension and accumulation exceeds AUD 3 million. (One research dimension, which had no live search access, described enactment as unresolved; the verified position above supersedes it.)

**Non-concessional contributions.** The cap is AUD 130,000 for 2026-27 (AUD 390,000 bring-forward under 75 with TSB below AUD 1.84 million) and nil where TSB at 30 June 2026 was AUD 2.1 million or more.

**SIS compliance for listed securities.** SIS Regulation 4.09 requires a written investment strategy considering risk and return, diversification, liquidity relative to cash-flow needs (including pension payments), ability to discharge liabilities and whether to hold insurance; it requires diversification to be considered, not achieved. Auditors check the strategy exists, addresses each factor, was reviewed and matches the holdings; since the ATO's 2019 letters to about 17,700 funds, portfolios over 90% in one asset class draw scrutiny. ASX-listed ETFs, LICs, exchange-traded bonds and hybrids bought on-market from unrelated issuers are not in-house assets and do not engage section 66 (which permits acquiring listed securities from a related party only at market value). The other live touchpoints are: holding assets in the trustee's name for the fund and separate from personal assets (Regulation 4.09A: a Holder Identification Number (HIN) and bank account in the fund's name); the trust deed permitting the investment; the sole purpose test (section 62); market valuation each 30 June (Regulation 8.02B, satisfied by ASX closing prices); the prohibition on borrowing and charges over fund assets (section 67, so no margin lending; internally geared ETFs do not themselves breach it but must be justified in the strategy); the 45-day franking rule; and, from 2026-27, Division 296 attribution for members above AUD 3 million.

**Record keeping.** Accounting records, contract notes and Attribution Managed Investment Trust (AMIT) Member Annual (AMMA) statements for at least 5 years; investment strategy and its reviews, trustee minutes and declarations for at least 10 years. Under the AMIT regime the AMMA statement, not the cash received, determines the franked, unfranked, capital gain and foreign income components, and the "AMIT cost base net amount" adjusts unit cost bases each year; a 100% ECPI fund should still record these for its accounts and audit.

## 2. How a AUD 10-25k parcel is executed in practice

**Broker accounts.** Every mainstream Australian online broker offers SMSF accounts and all of the following are CHESS-sponsored (Clearing House Electronic Subregister System), meaning holdings sit on the fund's own HIN at the share registry. Under a custodial (omnibus HIN) model the auditor cannot verify holdings by searching the fund's HIN at the registry and must rely on the custodian's controls report, failing which the Part A audit opinion may be qualified. Account opening typically needs the fund's Australian Business Number (mandatory), tax file number, trust deed (at least the schedule and execution pages), corporate trustee Australian Company Number and director IDs where applicable, identity verification for every trustee or director, and a bank account in the fund's name; CommSec quotes 2-5 business days.

**Trading mechanics.** The ASX minimum marketable parcel of AUD 500 applies to the first purchase of a security on a HIN; later top-ups can be smaller where the broker allows. Settlement is T+2 (two business days). ASX's stated preference is a move to T+1 no earlier than 2030, at least 12 months after CHESS Release 2 (targeted 2029); no T+1 date has been set. ETF best practice from ASX, Betashares and Morningstar: trade between roughly 10:30am and 3:30pm Sydney time (away from the opening and closing auctions), use limit orders, and compare the quote with the indicative net asset value (iNAV), which many broker sites show by prefixing "Y" to the ETF code (for example YSTW). Stockspot's June 2026 data show VAS with the lowest average bid-ask spread among Australian share ETFs at about 0.02% (a widely repeated AUD 107 million daily-turnover figure for VAS could not be substantiated and appears to be its unit price). IOZ trades at a 1-cent spread (about 0.03%) with roughly AUD 16-29 million a day; IAF at 0.03-0.10% with AUD 4-14 million a day; BILL, AAA and MMKT at 0.01-0.06%; HGBL at about 0.04%; CRED and HBRD in the 0.05-0.20% range. For every surviving candidate a AUD 25,000 order is a fraction of a percent of a normal day's turnover.

**Where "any broker" does not hold.** Exchange-traded Treasury Bonds (eTBs) and Exchange-traded Treasury Indexed Bonds (eTIBs) trade on ASX only, in AUD 100 face-value units (eTIB face value is CPI-indexed), minimum one unit, T+2, with AOFM-appointed market makers and no management fee, but they must be bought through a broker on the ASX-published list that supports exchange-traded Australian Government Bonds; several low-cost app brokers do not. Prices are quoted gross (accrued interest included); GSBG27 was estimated at about AUD 102 per unit and GSIQ30 at about AUD 150-156 (both unverified). XTBs (Australian Corporate Bond Company units) were suspended from Cboe Australia on 1 September 2023 and their admission revoked around 24 November 2023 after the issuer entered administration and then liquidation; they are no longer purchasable. Listed Investment Companies and listed investment trusts (LITs) trade at premiums or discounts to NTA: ARG turns over about AUD 4 million a day at a 1-cent spread, while smaller LICs and the private-credit LITs (MXT, GCI, KKC, PCI, PCX, 7-10% headline yields but credit and liquidity risk) were not liquidity-checked.

**Distributions and cash flow.** Distribution reinvestment plans (DRPs) are elected at the registry (Betashares via MUFG Corporate Markets, Vanguard via Computershare) and can be partial for many funds; a fund paying a pension generally takes cash. ETF distributions are AMIT attributions that vary with underlying dividends, realised gains and (for hedged funds) currency-hedging gains, so cash income is not stable in the way a term deposit is. The cash yield of most equity ETFs (1.2-3.3%) is below the 4% minimum, so a series of small unit sales, or a cash bucket, is required regardless of which growth product is held.

**Structural checks before buying (knowledge, not live-verified).** Confirm the listing venue (ASX versus Cboe Australia; all twelve surviving candidates are ASX-quoted), whether the issuer lends securities (BlackRock discloses lending with the fund keeping 62.5-82% of revenue; Vanguard Australia and Betashares have historically said their Australian funds do not lend, but current PDSs should be checked), and fund size: responsible entities may wind up sub-scale funds (a rule of thumb is under AUD 20-50 million), which forces reinvestment even though a wind-up has no tax cost for a 100% ECPI fund.

## 3. Risk framing and what mainstream Australian sources suggest for this age

Each item is labelled **Evidence** (verified primary or issuer data) or **Opinion** (commentary, consensus or the researchers' own judgment).

**Longevity (Evidence, partly unverified).** Australian Government Actuary life tables 2020-22 give a 60-year-old roughly 24 (male) to 27 (female) further years on a period basis, and cohort life expectancy is higher; about 38% of Australians survive past 90. The exact life-table figures were not confirmed against aga.gov.au. No ASX-listed product provides a lifetime income guarantee; lifetime annuities are unlisted (Opinion, not verified).

**Sequence-of-returns risk (Evidence for the definition; Opinion for mitigants).** Morningstar defines the "retirement risk zone" as the five years before and after retirement. Its 2026 Australian article on sequencing risk lists flexible spending after poor early returns, bucketing, drawdown-rate guidance, glidepath design and the Age Pension (which rises as account balances fall) as mitigants. ASIC Moneysmart states an account-based pension does not guarantee an income for life, continues only until the balance runs out, and that the member chooses the investment mix.

**Withdrawal rates (Evidence, with a correction).** Morningstar's "State of Retirement Income 2025" (December 2025, United States data) puts the highest safe starting withdrawal rate at 3.9% for a 30-year inflation-adjusted retirement at 90% success (30-50% equity portfolios), with flexible strategies lifting it to roughly 5.7%. A claim that a 2026 Morningstar Australia article argues for 3.3% was refuted: 3.3% was Morningstar's 2021 figure. Vanguard Australia favours a dynamic spending rule (annual ceiling about +5%, floor about -2%) over a rigid 4% rule, and its Australian modelling supports an initial rate around 5% on a 50/50 portfolio over 35 years; the "4.5-5%" range should be read as an approximation. Note (Opinion) that the legislated minimum (4% at 60, rising with age) is a floor, not a recommendation, and exceeds these estimates at older ages.

**Growth/defensive splits (Evidence).** AustralianSuper's Balanced option, the default for Choice Income pension accounts, targets around 70% growth assets, counting listed property and infrastructure as 100% growth and unlisted property, unlisted infrastructure and credit as 50/50. Australian Retirement Trust's Lifecycle strategy holds about 85% growth in its High Growth Pool until age 50 then shifts gradually to Balanced and Cash pools; this is an accumulation default, not a pension product. Industry-fund retirement defaults therefore cluster around 50-70% growth (Opinion drawn from the evidence).

**Bucket strategy (Opinion, labelled as such by the sources).** Colonial First State, SuperGuide and Morningstar describe holding one to three years of net spending in cash, a medium bucket of bonds or credit and a long bucket of growth assets, refilled from gains. There is no robust evidence that bucketing raises total returns versus a rebalanced total-return portfolio; it is a liquidity and behavioural framework. At a 4% minimum, a 2-3 year cash bucket is 8-12% of the pension balance, so one AUD 10-25k parcel covers a full bucket only for balances up to roughly AUD 300,000 (Opinion, arithmetic).

**Home bias and concentration (Evidence).** As at 30 June 2026 Financials were 33.3% and Materials 25.5% of the S&P/ASX 200 (about 59% combined); the top 10 stocks were about 47% (State Street STW fact sheet, 31 January 2026); Australia is roughly 1.6-2% of world equity market capitalisation. Betashares reported that at end-July 2025 about 55% of S&P/ASX 200 dividends came from ten companies; the index's trailing cash dividend yield has compressed to roughly 3.3-3.4% (Motley Fool August 2025; Morningstar 2026) from a 10-year average of about 4.3%. Stockspot and Betashares research argue for 40-60% international share exposure (Opinion).

**Currency (Evidence for the sources' positions).** Vanguard Australia and Morningstar Australia both state international bonds should generally be currency-hedged to keep their defensive role (Morningstar: unhedged global bond maximum drawdown above 29% versus about 11% hedged), whereas hedging international equities is a risk-management choice with negligible long-run return impact; unhedged global shares have historically lost less than hedged ones when equities fell and the AUD weakened at the same time (Vanguard). No AUD-hedged global bond ETF was researched, which is a gap.

**Rate backdrop (Evidence).** The RBA cash rate target is 4.35%, held at the 11 August 2026 meeting after three increases earlier in 2026 (media release mr-26-19); the next decision is 29 September 2026 and early-September market pricing put roughly 70% on a further 25 basis point rise. Trimmed mean inflation was 3.6% (July 2026). The 10-year Commonwealth bond yield reached 5.22% on 2 September 2026, its highest since 2011. Best 12-month term deposits were about 5.40-5.55% (Heartland 5.55%, AMP 5.40%; ANZ 5.25%, NAB 5.15-5.25%, Westpac 5.05%). Implication (Opinion): cash and floating-rate instruments pay roughly 4.4-4.8% gross with negligible duration, while fixed-rate bond ETFs have taken mark-to-market losses in 2026 but now carry yields to maturity near 5%. A gap-fill that ran without live access could not re-verify the 4.35% figure and noted only the February 2026 rise to 3.85% from knowledge; the verified RBA release above stands.

**Product-specific evidence relevant to a retiree.** High-yield screens overweight banks and miners whose payouts are cyclical (BHP's FY25 dividend was its lowest since FY2016-17). Headline yields can be inflated by realised capital gains (SYI's AUD 1.32 July 2026 distribution was only 9.03% franked) or option premium (YMAX 42.4% franked, 0.64% fee). Dividend-harvest strategies can erode capital (HVST). LIC discounts have widened to 10-23% (AFI 17.3%, ARG 15.3-18.2%, BKI 10.4%, WHF 22.6% in July-August 2026) and can widen further; LIC dividends are smoothed from reserves and lag portfolio performance both ways. Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) removes AT1 hybrid eligibility from 1 January 2027, with about AUD 44 billion of bank hybrids expected to be redeemed at first call through 2032; Moneysmart continues to describe hybrids as complex and loss-absorbing. ASIC media release 26-193MR (13 August 2026) warned that some online brokers market short-dated options, futures and fractional shares to retail investors with weak onboarding and disclosure.

## 4. Three illustrative ways a series of AUD 10-25k parcels could be combined

These are examples of structure, built only from the surviving candidates, to show how the pieces fit. They are not recommendations, do not consider any person's circumstances, and would need to be documented in the fund's own Regulation 4.09 investment strategy. Percentages are of the money being deployed, not of a whole fund.

**Example 1: "Cash bucket plus two cores" (roughly 50/50 growth/defensive).** One or two parcels of a cash ETF (AAA or BILL) sized to about two years of the 4% minimum; one parcel of IAF for intermediate-duration ballast; one parcel of a single Australian core (A200 or IOZ) for franked income; one parcel of BGBL for unhedged global equity. Four or five trades, blended fee roughly 0.08-0.10%. The cash bucket funds pension payments so the equity parcels are never forced sales; distributions from A200/IOZ plus refunded franking (about 4.0-4.2% grossed-up) and IAF (about 3%) partly refill the bucket. Trade-off: about half the money earns cash or bond returns close to inflation.

**Example 2: "Franked-income tilt" (roughly 60-65% growth).** One parcel of a cash ETF; one parcel of CRED for monthly unfranked credit income; one parcel of ARG as the Australian equity core (100% franked, about 6.0% grossed-up on current prices, quarterly from January 2027) instead of an index ETF; one parcel of BGBL and one of HGBL for a half-hedged global sleeve. Trade-off: ARG's 16-18% discount to NTA adds a second, uncontrollable price driver on top of equity risk; CRED's 5.8-year duration means both "defensive" parcels can fall together in a rate shock; international income is thin and its withholding tax is lost.

**Example 3: "Growth-heavy with satellites" (roughly 70-75% growth, closer to an industry-fund Balanced pension default).** One parcel of MMKT or BILL as the liquidity tier; one parcel of IAF; two parcels across A200/IOZ (one only) and BGBL; one parcel of HGBL or VGAD for currency-hedged global equity; one parcel of IFRA as a 5-10% AUD-hedged infrastructure satellite. Trade-off: the highest sequence-of-returns exposure of the three; IFRA is still listed equity and rate-sensitive; three international parcels lose roughly 0.2-0.3% a year to unrecoverable withholding; more line items mean more AMMA statements and more Regulation 4.09 documentation.

Common observations (Opinion): hold only one of A200/IOZ/VAS/STW and only one cash ETF; take distributions in cash rather than reinvesting; at 0% tax there is no reason to prefer distributions over unit sales, so "income" products earn a place only if their total return and cost justify it; and the unit counts in Part A show that every parcel is far above the AUD 500 minimum marketable parcel.

## 5. Can this be automated in Python?

**Feasible and already implemented.** The companion `smsf_screener.py` in this repository (see `SMSF_SCREENER.md`) implements the split the research recommends: a live price-and-distribution fetch from Yahoo Finance's chart endpoint using only the Python standard library by default (`--fetch-backend yahoo`; the third-party `yfinance` package, version 1.7.0 on PyPI as at 2026-09-06, is an optional alternative via `--fetch-backend yfinance`), plus a hand-curated `smsf_universe.csv` holding `mer_pct`, `size_aud_m`, `franking_pct` and an `as_at` date that triggers staleness warnings after 400 days. ASX tickers use the `.AX` suffix. Daily close and volume for mainstream ETFs, LICs and eTBs are reliable enough for volatility, drawdown and value-traded metrics. Several rows in the universe file were found stale by the fact-checks (for example IFRA's fee still at 0.52%, VGAD's size at 3,500, A200's size at 9,000, VDCO's franking at 20, DHHF's frequency as quarterly, BILL's size as 2,500); those rows were corrected from the fact-checks after this draft was written, and the universe's `as_at` column records the date.

**Feasible with caveats.** Yahoo's distribution history for ASX ETFs is patchy and fund-specific (some distributions missing, misdated or DRP-adjusted), while listed-company dividends are generally complete; the screener already compares distribution counts against stated frequency. Yahoo's terms are personal use only and the endpoint is unofficial, so breakage risk is permanent for both backends. The ASX website's Markit Digital feed (`asx.api.markitdigital.com/asx-research/1.0/companies/{code}/header` and `/key-statistics`) is scriptable without an API key and is used by the finance-quote project and several 2026 open-source projects, but it is undocumented, some sibling endpoints need a public token lifted from the ASX site's JavaScript that rotates without notice, and the terms of use are restrictive; the old `www.asx.com.au/asx/1/share/` endpoint is gone (an earlier claim that the Markit endpoint sits behind a CAPTCHA since February 2024 was refuted). The ASX Investment Products monthly report is free, published roughly 7-10 business days after month-end, covers of the order of 550-600 securities with market capitalisation, flows, performance, an average bid/ask spread measure and volume, and ships as a PDF plus a spreadsheet whose file naming and column layout have changed between years, so a parser needs a hand-maintained column map and a monthly download step; whether it carries a management-cost column for every product was not verified.

**Must be hand-maintained.** Management fees (from PDSs and issuer pages), fund size and franking levels. Per-distribution franking components appear only in "Estimated distribution component information" ASX announcements (two-page PDFs) and annual AMMA statements, not in any free structured feed. Betashares fund pages show expandable distribution tables with yield and franking but there is no documented public API (people scrape the HTML); Vanguard, iShares, VanEck and Global X publish holdings and distribution downloads behind undocumented URLs that change without notice. ATO constants (drawdown factors, TBC, thresholds, tax rates) change at most annually and belong in a dated constants table.

**Cannot be done reliably.** Live iNAV and bid/ask depth without a paid feed; LIC discounts to NTA without parsing weekly NTA announcements; forward yields for cash ETFs; and any judgment about suitability. Open-source prior art (ozacas/asxtrade, last commit February 2023; jericmac/pyasx, last commit 2019 and not on PyPI, using retired ASX endpoints; illi4/stock-screener, maintained but stocks-only with a paid data key) offers no maintained ETF screener with fees or franking.

**Licensing boundary (knowledge for the advice dimension, not live-verified).** Under Corporations Act 2001 section 766B(4) any financial product advice that is not personal advice is general advice; ASIC Regulatory Guide 244 treats objectively ascertainable information as factual information, not advice; section 949A requires a general advice warning to retail clients; RG 255 (August 2016) governs digital advice; and ASIC's generic-calculator relief (Instrument 2016/207, reported as remade in 2026 as Instrument 2026/41 but not verified) does not cover a tool that names and ranks specific products. A trustee running a transparent, rule-based screener for their own fund provides no financial service to anyone. Publishing a tool that scores named products for others is at least general advice, and embedding a user's age, phase and parcel size risks being personal advice under section 766B(3)(b), requiring an AFS licence. A dated screener print-out with its rules can be attached to trustee minutes as evidence that diversification, liquidity, fees and income were considered when reviewing the Regulation 4.09 strategy; it does not itself satisfy the regulation.

## 6. Questions a licensed adviser or SMSF specialist should be asked

1. Has the member met a retirement condition of release under SIS Regulation 6.01(7) and notified the trustee, so the pension is genuinely in retirement phase (0% tax) rather than a transition to retirement income stream (15%)?
2. What is the member's personal transfer balance cap after the 1 July 2026 indexation, how much cap space remains, and would any further pension commencement or reversionary pension breach it?
3. Does the fund need an actuarial certificate for 2026-27 (any accumulation interest, any member with total super balance above AUD 1.6 million receiving a pension), and which ECPI method applies?
4. Is the member's total super balance anywhere near AUD 3 million, and if so should the fund make the irrevocable Division 296 cost-base reset election for assets held at 30 June 2026 by the due date of the 2026-27 return?
5. How should the fund's written investment strategy document liquidity for the 4% minimum (rising to 5% at 65), the chosen growth/defensive split, currency hedging and any concentration in banks and miners, so the auditor is satisfied under Regulation 4.09?
6. Given the fund pays no tax, is the loss of foreign withholding tax on international ETFs (roughly 0.2-0.3% a year) material enough to change the mix between Australian and international holdings?
7. Should franked income be sourced from an index ETF, a LIC trading at a 16-18% discount to NTA, or direct shares, and how does the 45-day holding rule interact with any planned rebalancing?
8. How should pension payments be scheduled (monthly, quarterly, annual lump sum) so that cash from distributions, franking refunds (which arrive 6-18 months later) and unit sales arrives in time, and what cash buffer does that imply?
9. Are commutations planned (which debit the transfer balance account, do not count towards the minimum and must be reported on a quarterly Transfer Balance Account Report), and how does that interact with the Age Pension assets and income tests?
10. What estate arrangements apply: binding death benefit nomination, reversionary pension and the recipient's own cap, tax on the taxable component paid to non-dependants, and trustee succession?
11. Which broker structure (CHESS-sponsored HIN in the fund's name, corporate versus individual trustee eligibility, eTB support, Cboe support) suits the fund, and what does the linked cash account pay?
12. If the trustee builds or uses a screening tool, what wording and disclaimers keep it factual information for the trustee's own use, and what changes if it is shared with anyone else?
